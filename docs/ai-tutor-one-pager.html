AI Tutor — how it answers from the textbook

Prepared for: Engineering manager | From: Mayur | As of: 18 Sep 2026 Branch: feature/english-only-latency-opti | Device: Intel i5-1145G7, 4 cores, 8 GB, offline

Bottom line. The tutor runs fully offline on a 4-core school laptop. A 1.5B model answers each question from passages of the student's own textbook, found by searching on the laptop itself. In a 54-turn run on the target laptop on 18 Sep, the first word of an answer arrived in a median 2.8 s, against 11.7 s on 8 Sep, and a follow-up that stays on the same passage answered in about 1.3 s. The corpus now on devices has 89% of answer claims backed by the passage the model read, up from 71%. The main limit is still retrieval, not the model: only about half of the book passages that answer a question reach the prompt.

1. The system at a glance

The work splits in two. Each textbook becomes a searchable file once, on a fast machine, because embedding a whole book is too slow for a school laptop. That file ships with the installer. On the laptop, with no network, the backend finds the closest passages for each question and gives them to a small model running in Ollama.

The only thing that crosses onto the laptop is a file. The installer pins the Ollama version and ships the models, so inference behaves the same on every device in the fleet.

2. From PDF to a searchable library

This stage decides retrieval quality. A textbook page is more than the lesson. Exercises, activity boxes and figure captions cover the same topic, so they land right next to the real definition in the index and compete with it.

Steps 4 and 5 are where this month's groundedness work went. The exercise filter took the index from 447 to 327 chunks without losing any of the 69 answer phrases in the two evaluation sets. Reflow cut mid-sentence breaks from 2,365 to 26, and definitions kept whole rose from 38 to 102 of 103. The corpus was rebuilt with both changes on 18 Sep and now ships 373 chunks.

Why step 5 exists. Removed, from the p.121 end-of-chapter exercise:

(c) There is repulsion between the .......... poles of a magnet, and attraction between its ............ poles.

Kept, from the p.118 lesson:

There is repulsion between like poles of a magnet, while there is attraction between the opposite poles.

Before the filter, the exercise line ranked first for "What are the poles of a magnet?". The model filled the blanks in the order the options were printed and taught that like poles attract. The two sentences are near-identical in embedding space, so no distance threshold can separate them — the filter has to act before the text is embedded.

3. Finding the passages (RAG)

The search itself takes milliseconds. The real cost is what it returns: the model has to read every character sent, at roughly 0.4 s per 100 characters on the target laptop. So the pipeline controls both which passages come back and how much of each is sent.

Why the "yes" branch exists. Searched alone, "How can we reduce it?" retrieved soil erosion (p.17) and the balanced-diet pyramid (p.64). Both passed the distance gate, because a question with no topic is a mediocre match for everything. Searched as "What is frictional force? How can we reduce it?", it finds the friction section at distance 0.21. A new topic is never searched with context: "What is photosynthesis?" carried after a chemistry question once matched "atomic number".

  • Grade is a partition key. A Class 6 question searches only Class 6 vectors. If the subject picked in the app matches nothing, the search retries across the whole grade.
  • The 0.42 gate is calibrated. Questions the text answers scored 0.12–0.34, off-topic ones 0.50–0.58. An off-syllabus question retrieves nothing, and abstention held in every eval run.
  • Search is dense-only, as a flat scan. On 18 Sep, search plus query embedding took a median 75 ms, under 1% of a turn, and both passages cleared the gate on all 50 turns.

4. Generating the answer

On this CPU the model reads new prompt text at about 16 ms per token and writes at 16.6 tokens a second. Reading the prompt is 93% of the wait for the first word, though only about a third of the whole turn now that answers run to about 100 words. So the prompt is built to be short and to repeat itself: Ollama skips the work for any leading run of tokens identical to its previous request, which is why the prompt runs from what never changes to what changes every turn.

Totals and first-word times are medians from the 18 Sep device run; the split between persona, excerpts and question is approximate. Six of eight follow-ups kept their passage and so kept the cache; the two that retrieved a different passage paid full price, like a new question.

  • Excerpts stay in the system message. Moving them next to the question was tried twice and reverted: only 5 of 12 follow-ups stayed on the conversation, against 11 of 12 with the excerpts in the system message.
  • What the model reads is not what the student sees. The full reply is streamed, stored and shown, but never sent back to the model. A follow-up carries only the earlier question, plus the length rule spelled out again.
  • Streaming and staying warm. The pages used are sent to the app before the first token, then tokens stream over SSE, and the browser starts reading the answer aloud at a median 4.1 s. Model load cost 57–83 ms on every turn, instead of a ~2 s reload after a pause.

6. To work on next: sanitise the book before ingestion

Retrieval, not the model, is the ceiling on groundedness. When the right passage reached the model, 25% of answers had an error, against 48% when it didn't.

What still goes wrong (observed)

  • Wrong section labels. The heading is embedded with every chunk, so a wrong label pulls text toward the wrong questions. On p.40 the text on serious burns is filed under "Snakebite", and chapter 8's skeleton pages under "Food Items Adulterants".
  • Columns read out of order. pypdf interleaves the two columns on some pages, so p.83's text on electrostatic force lands under "Frictional force".
  • Rules fitted to one book. Exercise detection matches this publisher's own phrasing ("Fill in the blanks", "Use your brain power!") and a 0.40 page threshold measured on these 132 pages. A different board's book will need re-tuning.
  • Half of the answers never reach the prompt. Context recall is 51%. Chunk boundaries are the limit now, not noise. The 18 Sep run retrieved two passages on every turn, so passages are arriving; the question is whether they hold the answer.

Proposed work, in suggested order

  1. Explain the fall in facts covered. The v4 corpus is already on devices, so repeat the v2 and v4 runs to separate sampling noise from a real regression on what students use now.
  2. Layout-aware extraction. Use word positions and font sizes (e.g. PyMuPDF) so columns come out in reading order and headings are recognised by type size rather than guessed from text shape.
  3. Tag content, don't only delete it. Label each paragraph as definition, explanation, activity, exercise or summary. Hard-delete only blanks and true/false lists, and down-rank the rest at search time.
  4. Gate every new book. Run the answer-phrase check, gold-set check and offline recall replay on each ingest, and fail the build if any answer is lost. The scripts exist, but so far only for this book.

7. Two approaches to evaluate

Both leave retrieval untouched and aim at the model's side of the errors: it sometimes contradicts a passage it read (joints are "of two types" became three) and it follows the answer-shape rules loosely.

A. Edge-device model: Qwen3-1.7B in place of qwen2.5:1.5b

A newer model of the same size should make fewer grounding errors at almost the same speed.

  • Same tokenizer and chat format, and it loads on the pinned Ollama, so the prompt, budget and tests carry over unchanged.
  • Ollama's stock Qwen3 template breaks follow-up caching. We would ship a derived model with a corrected template, which measured 94%.
  • The two projections are the 18 Sep device medians scaled by dev-machine ratios for this model (prefill ×1.05–1.18, writing ×0.82–0.86). Nothing has been run on the device yet.
  • Already ruled out: LFM2.5-1.2B (no cache reuse on Ollama 0.12.3, 7 errors in 20 against 4), and every 3–4B model (projected 25–45 s per turn).

How we decide: a side-by-side run on the school laptop in ABBA order, using the benchmark and the groundedness eval. Adopt only if wrong answers drop and first word rises by no more than 0.5 s. Runner-up: Granite 4.0 1B, as a Q4 build.

B. Style fine-tune: teach the answer shape with a small LoRA

Putting the answer shape in the weights should make it reliable in a way that prompt wording hasn't.

Why the prompt alone isn't enough (measured)

  • The style rule went through five versions, and replies swung between 223 and 851 characters.
  • 6 of 90 answers still used headings or bullets despite an explicit ban (target).
  • 9 of 32 follow-ups came back as a single sentence, even with the length rule restated (Mac).
  • On the device, 18 Sep: answers ran to a median 472 characters against the 550–700 the rule asks for, and 4 of 50 came in under 200 (target).

The idea

  • Train on the base we choose from the model A/B. Each example pairs a textbook excerpt and a question with an answer that uses only that excerpt, in the house shape: 5–6 plain sentences, one everyday example, no headings. Include follow-ups and cases where the excerpt doesn't answer.
  • Facts stay in retrieval. The fine-tune teaches the shape and staying on the excerpt, not science content.
  • Merge the adapter, quantise to Q4_K_M and ship it as an Ollama model. The architecture is unchanged, so speed and cache behaviour are too.

Risks

  • A fine-tune can weaken faithfulness or instruction following, so it has to match or beat its base on the groundedness eval.
  • It needs a few hundred reviewed examples across chapters, kept separate from the eval questions.
  • The speed gain is small, because the style rule already sits in the cached prefix. The payoff is consistency.

How we decide: run it after the model choice, so there is only one training run. Same gates as the model swap, plus format checks (sentence count, no headings) on questions held out from training.
